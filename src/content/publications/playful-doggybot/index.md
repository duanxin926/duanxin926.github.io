## Abstract

Quadrupedal animals can perform agile and playful tasks while interacting with real-world objects. For instance, a trained dog can track and catch a flying frisbee before it touches the ground, while a cat left alone at home may leap to grasp the door handle. Successfully grasping an object during high-dynamic locomotion requires highly precise perception and control. However, due to hardware limitations, agility and precision are usually a trade-off in robotics problems. 

In this work, we employ a perception-control decoupled system based on Reinforcement Learning (RL), aiming to explore the level of precision a quadrupedal robot can achieve while interacting with objects during high-dynamic locomotion. Our experiments show that our quadrupedal robot, mounted with a passive gripper in front of the robot's chassis, can perform both tracking and catching tasks similar to a real trained dog. 

The robot can follow a mid-air ball moving at speeds of up to 3m/s and it can leap and successfully catch a small object hanging above it at a height of 1.05m in simulation and 0.8m in the real world.

## Key Features

- **High-Dynamic Locomotion**: Robot can track and catch objects while moving at high speeds
- **Agile Jumping**: Successfully catches objects at 1.05m high in simulation and 0.8m in the real world
- **Perception-Control Decoupled System**: Based on Reinforcement Learning for precise control
- **Real-World Performance**: Validated in laboratory experiments
